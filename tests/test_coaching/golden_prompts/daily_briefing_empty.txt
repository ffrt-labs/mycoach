## Steps (7 days ending yesterday)

Steps rules:
- A 7-day average at or above the target means "maintain". Do not ask for more steps; redirect extra effort to Zone 2 and strength.
- Ignore single low days. Treat changes within +/-10% as noise (wrist step counts are about that accurate). A weekend-heavy pattern that still meets the weekly volume is fine.
- Comment on steps only if the 7-day average is more than 15% below the previous 7-day average, or there are 3 or more consecutive days below 50% of the 7-day average. In that case, ask about the cause first (illness, injury, travel, schedule) before prescribing anything.
- Say "associated with", never diagnose.

## Body (weight and waist)
No body measurements logged.

Body rules (the numbers above are computed; interpret them, never recompute them):
- Never comment on a single day's weight; use the weight trend (2+ weeks). A jump of 1-2 kg is water: look at the notes (salt, carbs, alcohol, travel, a hard session).
- Under 0.3 kg/week, or a waist change of 1 cm or less, means no detectable change. Losing more than 1% of bodyweight per week is too aggressive.
- Waist down with weight flat is a win (recomposition). Waist up with weight flat is worth raising gently.
- Frame risk with waist-to-height (target under 0.50), never BMI. Never diagnose; suggest a DEXA scan if body composition detail is wanted.
- Mention weight or waist only when something changed meaningfully or a nudge is due.

## How to read HRV
- The primary signal is the HRV 7-day avg against the athlete's own range (Garmin's baseline band). Never prescribe from one night; last night is context only.
- Treat changes under about 10% as noise.
- A drop counts only when it agrees with resting HR at least 5 bpm above its recent level, or with poor sleep. A drop under high training load is expected fatigue; a drop without load suggests illness, stress or alcohol: ask, don't prescribe.
- Low status, or 3+ days below the Low threshold, means a recovery block; ask about illness.
- Unbalanced above the range together with elevated resting HR or a recent heavy block means possible overreaching.
- Never compare HRV to other people or population norms. If no range is listed, Garmin is still building the baseline: say so and don't infer from HRV.
