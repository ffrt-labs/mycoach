## Coaching principles

Source: Huberman Lab Neural Network Digest Vol. 1 (Toolkit for Sleep; Foundational Fitness Protocol; The Science & Use of Cold Exposure). Apply a principle only when the athlete's data gives a reason (the trigger after "When:"); principles with no trigger are background, mentioned at most briefly and not every day. Never repeat a principle the athlete is already following.

Sleep
- Wake at the same time every day, weekends too; go to bed when first sleepy. When: wake times in the health data vary by more than about 1 h, or sleep score drops after a late night. Nudge toward one anchored wake time.
- Get outside light within 30-60 min of waking and again in the late afternoon; dim bright lights late at night. Advice only, no trigger.
- No caffeine 8-10 h before bed. When: poor sleep score or long awake time and the athlete mentions caffeine or late workouts with pre-workout drinks.
- Alcohol degrades sleep. When: the athlete reports drinking and sleep score or HRV drops.
- Sleep in a cool, dark room with removable layers. When: repeated night waking or high awake time.
- Keep naps under 90 min, or skip them. When: the athlete reports long naps alongside poor night sleep.
- Feeling alert about 1 h before usual bedtime is normal and passes; do not push past the first sleepiness after it.
- NSDR (10-30 min non-sleep deep rest) after a poor night or night waking, and when unable to fall back asleep. When: low sleep score, short sleep or high awake time.

Timing
- No hard resistance training late in the evening. When: sleep score or HRV drops on nights after evening strength sessions.
- No ice bath or cold immersion 6-8 h after lifting; it blunts strength and hypertrophy gains. Cold showers are fine; cold before training is fine.

Cardio
- Weekly Zone 2 target 180-200 min, measured from the activities' HR zones (zone 2 time). When: weekly Zone 2 is well short of the target, suggest where to add it.
- HIIT only with movements the athlete can do in perfect form (bike, rower, sprints on a familiar surface).

In-session and recovery (post-workout)
- Physiological sigh between hard sets to lower heart rate: two nose inhales, one long mouth exhale.
- 3-5 min of slow breathing after a session to downshift.
- Static stretching: 30-60 s holds per major muscle group, at least 3 times a week, long exhales during the hold.

Cold exposure (optional, never required)
- About 11 min per week total over 2-4 sessions of 1-5 min, uncomfortably cold but safe; early in the day, not near bedtime; End with cold (let the body reheat on its own). Never on lifting days within 6-8 h after the session.

Travel
- Jet lag: live on the destination's clock from day one. Get outside light in the destination's morning and dim bright light in its late evening; apply the caffeine cutoff on local time; schedule hard training in the local morning or afternoon, never late in the local evening. When: travel with a time-zone shift of 3 h or more.

Illness and stress
- Mild cold symptoms: train at 50% or less of usual duration or intensity, or skip; truly sick: skip. After recovery, ramp back up over 3-7 days.
- Poor sleep or acute stress: consider skipping the session or substituting an easier one, and doing NSDR; make the work up later in the week.

Out of scope
- Never recommend a supplement or a dose. Only refer to one the athlete already reports taking (e.g. creatine), and never suggest a new one.
